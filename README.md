## The entry

> An echo is a wall playing catch with your voice — you throw a shout at it, and it throws the same shout back to you.
>
> It works because your shout really does travel to the wall and bounce back, but it leaves out that the wall isn't catching anything — your voice comes back quieter and a little scrambled, and the wall never chooses to answer.

Two lines. Line 1 explains an echo to a 10-year-old through one concrete, surprising analogy a child can test by shouting at a wall. Line 2 is honest about what the analogy maps (the shout travelling out and bouncing back) and what it leaves out (the wall catches nothing, the voice returns quieter and scrambled, and no choice is involved).

## How it runs

`main.py` (Python 3, standard library only) writes the two lines to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` — UTF-8, no trailing newline — and leaves the file readable by every user (0644, folders 0755).

```sh
MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=/output python3 main.py
```
